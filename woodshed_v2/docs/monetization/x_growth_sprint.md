# Woodshed X audience growth sprint

**Priority:** Build a larger, engaged fantasy-football audience first. Monetization is deferred.  
**Prepared:** 2026-10-15  
**Status:** Operating recommendation; no posts have been published.

## First, establish the baseline

The current X audience and analytics were not verified as part of this plan. On day one, record the visible follower count and the available analytics/export for the previous 28 days. Record the date and metric definitions. Do not promise a follower target before seeing that baseline.

Track weekly:

- Net new followers, profile visits, impressions, replies, reposts, and bookmarks when available.
- Follows per profile visit and substantive replies per original post.
- Newsletter-link clicks and confirmed opt-in signups, if a signup page exists.
- Performance by post format and topic, not just the best single post.
- Actual X growth separately from newsletter signups and any paid revenue.

Use the same weekly snapshot day and compare rolling 28-day periods. If analytics are unavailable, record that as not verified rather than estimating.

## Four-week rhythm

A sustainable starting cadence: one original post on five days each week, plus a short daily block for relevant replies. Use two or three 10-minute reply sessions on active game days. Adjust after two weeks based on time and response quality.

| Day | Original content job | Engagement action |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Weekend role/usage takeaway using verified, timestamped data | Reply to analysts discussing the same game with one additional piece of evidence |
| Tuesday | Waiver or role-change opinion with the main counterpoint | Ask one focused question that invites football disagreement |
| Wednesday | Standalone player/team usage read | Join relevant conversations early; do not tag people just to attract views |
| Thursday | Matchup/role angle or a VERSUS slate thesis | Reply to thoughtful disagreement; clarify the evidence or update the view |
| Friday | “What would change my view?” or a brief research recap | Invite readers to make a specific counterargument |
| Sunday (optional) | Live, verified observation only when there is a distinct point | Add context to an active conversation; avoid low-information play-by-play |

These are content jobs, not mandatory posts on every named day. Keep frequency sustainable and favor posts that offer a real analytical point.

## Editorial pattern for engagement

Lead with the actual football opinion. Follow it with the best verified usage evidence, the strongest counterargument, and a question a knowledgeable reader can answer. Use routes, snaps, targets, air yards, rush share, practice/injury status, and matchup context only when sourced and current.

Draft frames:

- “I’m still in on [player]. The role is why: [verified usage]. [Best counterpoint]. What would have to change for you to move off him?”
- “The part I can’t get past with [player] is [verified usage]. The box score says [verified result]; the role data says [Jon’s view]. Am I overweighting [counterevidence]?”
- “I changed my view on [player/team] after [verified change]. Here’s what moved it: [evidence]. What are you watching next?”

These are structures, not finished posts. Fill claims only after source review and Jon’s football judgment. Keep posts natural and specific; avoid bait questions, engagement pods, repetitive replies, unsupported player claims, fake urgency, and generic “agree?” prompts.

## Reuse and rights

- Start from independently researched Woodshed-original work.
- FFToday assignments remain commissioned work. Do not repurpose article copy, unpublished drafts, exclusive assignment research, or FFToday-only deliverables into X posts without permission.
- For overlapping players or games, build a separate Woodshed source trail and distinct analysis. If the content rights or timing are unclear, hold it.
- For a sponsored or affiliate mention, disclose the relationship clearly beside the endorsement. Keep paid messaging separate from independent analysis.

## Weekly review and learning loop

1. Save the analytics snapshot and link each post or note its publication time.
2. Identify the top two posts by meaningful interaction and the two that underperformed relative to the baseline.
3. Explain why each may have performed differently: topic relevance, evidence clarity, timing, conversation quality, and source audience. Treat this as a hypothesis, not causal proof.
4. Read replies for recurring questions, disagreements, and terms readers use. Turn those into next week’s content prompts.
5. Recommend one experiment at a time: a different opening, evidence format, reply angle, or timing.
6. Report results as actual platform metrics, with source and date. Do not report projections as growth.

After 28 days, review net follows, engagement per post, profile visits, and the quality of recurring conversations with Jon. Decide whether to keep the cadence, change content mix, or extend the audience-building phase. Only revisit paid newsletter testing when Jon approves a readiness gate based on actual audience growth and repeat interest. Do not choose that threshold from an assumed follower count.

## Approval boundary

Prepare posts and replies for Jon to review. Do not publish, schedule, DM, or contact potential collaborators without Jon’s explicit approval. Do not buy promotion or make an audience-size claim without a dated source.
